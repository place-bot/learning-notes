# Arithmetic, word manipulation, news generation and other abilities

## 1. Why join the synthesis task?

Training contamination and underlying task demonstrations of natural language benchmarks are difficult to fully measure. The author also constructs a controllable task to study whether the model can identify new rules from prompts:

- Addition and subtraction from two to six digits;
- two-digit multiplication;
- Multi-step single-digit operations;
- Word reverse order, letter cycle and anagram;
- Insert symbols into words;
- Use a newly coined word;
- Corrected grammar, analogies and general knowledge tasks.

## 2. Arithmetic

prompt provides several calculations and results:

```text
48 + 76 = 124
34 + 58 = 92
27 + 86 =
```

The performance of few-shot 3-digit addition and subtraction can be very strong; as the number of digits increases, the accuracy decreases, especially for six-digit operations and two-digit multiplication. Errors often include:

- A certain bit carry failed;
- The output is close to the correct number;
- Be more familiar with common number combinations;
- Example format changes lead to performance changes.

This shows that the model learned partial numerical patterns but did not form a stable, bit-independent accurate algorithm.

## 3. Word operation

Reverse word order:

```text
hello = olleh
world = dlrow
model =
```

The model infers character-level transformations from examples. The token boundaries of byte-level BPE may break a word into non-character granular fragments, so the Transformer needs to restore the character structure across tokens. Increasing size generally improves performance, but there are still significant failures on different tasks.

## 4. Make sentences with new words

The paper gives the model a newly coined word and its definition, and then requires it to be used in sentences. This type of task tests:

1. Temporarily bind new word semantics from the current context;
2. Generate grammatically correct sentences;
3. Keep definitions consistent.

The result is mainly a qualitative example, indicating that the model can be combined to utilize context definitions, but it cannot guarantee a stable overall success rate.

## 5. News generation experiment

The author gives the news title and subtitle, lets models of different sizes write articles of about 200 or 500 words, and then lets humans judge the authenticity. For a sample of about 200 words, the accuracy of identifying 175B GPT-3 text is about 52%, close to random guessing; for a sample of about 500 words, it is still about 52%.

The paper also reports that the human recognition rate of the control model is significantly higher, and the larger the model, the harder it is to recognize. This experiment measures the discriminability under specific prompts, sampling settings, and crowdsourced judgments, which does not mean that the news generated by the model is reliable or undetectable.

## 6. Generate fluency and authenticity

A text can satisfy:

\[
\text{style like news}
\]

But not satisfied:

\[
\text{The event is true}
\quad\land\quad
\text{consistent details}
\quad\land\quad
\text{Verifiable source}.
\]

It is difficult for humans to identify the source of generation but increases the risk of misuse, because the language model optimizes the probability of continuation, not fact verification.

## 7. Emergence of capabilities and robust capabilities

The synthesis task demonstrates the model's ability to ad hoc generalize patterns from a small number of examples. But to be called a robust algorithm, it also needs to be checked:

- Input length extrapolation;
- Counterfactual numbers and rare strings;
- prompt rewritten;
- Example order and error examples;
- combination of out-of-distribution rules;
- Sample variance multiple times.

The GPT-3 paper provides strong indications of emerging capabilities and clearly demonstrates that algorithm reliability is still insufficient.
