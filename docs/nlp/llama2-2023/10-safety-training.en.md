# Security fine-tuning, Context Distillation and Red Teaming

## 1. Three-layer security training

### 1.1 Safety SFT

Annotators design adversarial prompts and write responses that are safe, well-explained, and as helpful as possible, and are trained on general SFT data.

### 1.2 Safety RLHF

Collect more difficult attack prompts, let annotators compare the safety of multiple answers, train Safety RM, and then use it for rejection sampling and PPO.

### 1.3 Safety Context Distillation

First add security prepromt to the model:

```text
You are a safe and responsible assistant ...
```

Generate a safer answer \(y^+\), then use the original user prompt with \(y^+\) fine-tuning, without retaining preprompt:

\[
(c_{\mathrm{safety}},p)
\xrightarrow{\pi}
y^+,
\]

\[
(p,y^+)
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{SFT}}
\theta'.
\]

The behavior generated by the security context is distilled into parameters.

## 2. Risk Category

The paper is mainly divided into:

- illicit and criminal activities；
- hateful and harmful activities；
- unqualified advice, such as medical, financial, legal advice.

Attack vectors include psychological manipulation, false premises, spelling distortions, metaphors, role-playing, non-English languages, and long dialogue.

## 3. Safe Answer Guide

The target answers are usually in the order:

1. Address immediate security risks;
2. Explain why the request may be risky;
3. Provide safe alternatives, background knowledge, or recourse when possible;
4. Avoid meaningless preaching and excessive rejection.

The tension between safety and helpfulness is reflected in: after rejecting dangerous details, you should still try to respond to the user's legitimate purpose.

## 4. Safety data scaling

The paper keeps about 0.9M helpfulness samples unchanged, increases the safety data from 0% to 100%, the total amount is about 0.1M, and trains multiple variants.

The Safety RM score improves significantly with the increase of safety data, and the helpfulness score does not decrease significantly when supported by sufficient helpfulness data. However, the false refusal rate will increase, indicating that excessive security tuning will damage normal requests.

## 5. Why switch to RLHF after only doing a few thousand Safety SFTs?

The model quickly learns to write detailed rejections and alternative suggestions from a small number of safety demonstrations; the marginal value of continuing to write long answers manually decreases. Preference comparison is better suited to distinguish:

- Short rejections and helpful safe answers;
- Answers that appear safe but still reveal dangerous details;
- Excessive rejection and reasonable answers;
- Minor violations under multiple rounds of inducement.

## 6. Side effects of Context distillation

An appendix to the paper shows how it might:

- Add redundant security warnings to harmless questions;
- Misjudgment of context and rejection;
- Make answers templated;
- Solidify preprompt's bias into parameters.

The author lets Safety RM decide whether to use context distillation for each sample to avoid indiscriminate application to all prompts.

## 7. Red Team

The Red Team consists of more than 350 people, including experts in cybersecurity, election disinformation, law, policy, civil rights, ethics, software engineering, ML, Responsible AI, and creative writing, also spanning diverse demographic backgrounds.

Tests range from Criminal Planning, Human Trafficking, Controlled Substances, Explicit Content, Unqualified Advice, Privacy, Weapons and Cyber, and try out attacks such as Role Play, Creation Tasks, Aggressive Packaging, Spelling Variants, and Long Dialogue.

## 8. Red Team Closed Loop

Each discovery round is used for:

- fine-tuning data；
- model feedback training；
- safety model training；
- Next round of candidate release retesting.

The paper measures the difficulty of the red team by the number of violation prompts found per person per hour. An example from 7B drops from 1.8 to 0.45; the new model has an average rejection rate of about 90% for the previous round of violation prompts.

## 9. Evaluation boundaries

Red Teaming covers a broad but not exhaustive range of languages, cultures, attackers, and future attack combinations. Training into the red team may also lead to familiarity with the benchmark. Security requires continuous monitoring, deployment layer filtering, access control, and scenario testing, and cannot be borne solely by model parameters.
