# 14. Continuous reaction model

## 14.1 Current usage of continuous reaction format

**Condition in social science research:** Researchers rarely use continuous response format

**Example of continuous reaction:**

- Subjects were asked to describe their opinions on a scale of 1 to 100
- Subjects are asked to indicate a point by marking a line anchored from high to low
- Even responses in Likert-type item format can be considered an approximation of a continuous scale

## 14.2 Reasons to avoid continuous response formats

**Main Question:**

- Increased subjectivity due to scale expansion
- Generate reaction bias

## 14.3 IRT processing of continuous responses

**Contribution by Mellenbergh (1994b):**

- Continuous reactions can be easily handled by IRT models by treating them as observed manifestations of a latent variable
- In this case, factor analysis models can be used to understand item responses
