# 0. Introduction: Understanding Binary Data

This chapter introduces IRT models suitable for binary data. What is binary data? In binary data, item responses are divided into two categories, indicating success (1) or failure (0).

**Examples from real life:**

- Exam item: correct answer (1) or wrong answer (0)
- Questionnaire survey: "Do you like sports?" Yes (1) or No (0)
- Behavior observation: "Did the child cry today?" appears (1) or does not appear (0)

## 0.1 Two important characteristics of binary data

**The first feature: wide applicability**

Although ability or achievement items are prototypical binary items (i.e., true or false), many other types of data are applicable:

- Personality self-report (yes or no)
- Attitude agreement (agree or disagree)
- Behavior assessment scale (appearance and absence)

**The second feature: simplification of multi-category data**

In some cases, multi-category responses can be reduced to a binary format. For example:

- The four-point rating scale can be simplified to a binary format by merging the categories at both ends
- 1,2 → 0 (disagree)
- 3,4 → 1 (agree)

Important reminder

This approach is only appropriate if latent trait information is not lost (see Jansen & Roskam, 1986; Andrich, 1996).

If latent trait information is lost through binary scoring, the use of multinomial models should be considered (see Chapter 5).

**Judgment Criteria:** If there is indeed no difference in latent traits between people who originally "strongly agree" and "agree", the merger is reasonable; otherwise, the original categories should be retained.

## 0.2 Structure of this chapter

This chapter is divided into two main parts:

1. **Unidimensional IRT model**: where a single trait level represents individual differences
2. **Multidimensional IRT model**: where two or more trait levels represent individual differences

Although many new IRT models have been proposed over the past two decades, this chapter covers only a few major models of each type.
