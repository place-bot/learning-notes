# Definition of strict identifiability

## Definition 1

The paper defines the identity of \((\Theta,\boldsymbol p)\) as:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&P(\boldsymbol R=\boldsymbol r
\mid Q,\Theta,\boldsymbol p)
=
P(\boldsymbol R=\boldsymbol r
\mid Q,\bar\Theta,\bar{\boldsymbol p})
\quad
\forall\boldsymbol r\in\{0,1\}^J\\
&\hspace{3em}\Longleftrightarrow
(\Theta,\boldsymbol p)
=
(\bar\Theta,\bar{\boldsymbol p}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The left side indicates that the two sets of parameters generate exactly the same observed response distribution, and the right side requires that the two sets of parameters are identical item by item.

## Why is there no label swapping?

The latent classes of the ordinary latent class model are only numbered. Swapping the parameter columns and mixing ratios of the two categories does not change the observation distribution, so at most label substitutions are identified.

The column labels here have predetermined meanings:

\[
(0,0),\ (1,0),\ (0,1),\ (1,1)
\]

Represent specific attribute mastery states respectively, and the Q matrix also defines restrictions according to these attributes. Therefore, the paper requires strict equality under fixed semantic labels.

## strict and generic

|concept|quantifier|allowed set of failures|
| --- | --- | --- |
| strict identifiability |holds for every allowed parameter point in the parameter space|not allowed|
| generic identifiability |Established after removing the set with measure zero|Allow exception parameter points|
| local identifiability |There is no other equivalent point near a certain parameter point|There may still be equivalent points in the distance|

Xu's master theorem gives strict identifiability. C1 and C2 are sufficient conditions to ensure this strong conclusion.

## identifiability and estimated performance

Identifiability is a property at the overall distribution level. It assumes that we know the exact

\[
\left\{
P(\boldsymbol R=\boldsymbol r):
\boldsymbol r\in\{0,1\}^J
\right\}.
\]

Therefore:

- Identifiable instructions with unique parameters under unlimited information;
- Limited sample estimates may still have large variance;
- When conditions are close to failure, the likelihood surface may be very flat;
- The convergence of the optimization algorithm does not mean that it is theoretically identifiable;
- The theory can identify and does not guarantee that a certain optimization will reach the global optimum.

## Connection to estimability

If the model is not identifiable, then different parameter points correspond to the same distribution, and any estimation method based on response data cannot consistently select one of the true values.

If the model is strictly identifiable and satisfies conventional regularity, parameter space and likelihood conditions, maximum likelihood estimation can have a basis for establishing consistency. The paper gives this large sample connection after the main theorem. For details, see [From identifiability to consistency](15-consistency.md).

## Contact with CAT

Each student in CAT answers only a subset of adaptively selected questions. Xu's definition is based on the complete \(J\)-dimensional response distribution under fixed \(Q\) and does not directly deal with the adaptive missing mechanism.

Therefore, this theorem can guide the basic design of item bank and reaction model, but it cannot be directly derived:

- Parameters can still be identified under any adaptive policy;
- Each student's attribute profile can be accurately restored;
- The test length is short enough;
- There is still enough coverage after the dynamic item is exposed.

Applying this theory to CAT also requires studying the data support set generated by the topic selection strategy and its ignorability or exploration conditions.
