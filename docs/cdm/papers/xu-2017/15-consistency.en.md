# From identifiability to consistency

## Empirical marginal vector

For \(N\) independent participants, the paper considers the empirical full comparison ratio of all item subsets. remember

\[
\widehat\gamma_{\boldsymbol r}
=
\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
\mathbb I(\boldsymbol R_i\succeq\boldsymbol r),
\qquad
\boldsymbol r\in\{0,1\}^J.
\]

Arrange these quantities in \(T\)-matrix row order

\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol\gamma}.
\]

The law of large numbers gives

\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol\gamma}
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}
T(Q,\Theta)\boldsymbol p.
\]

## Fitting of maximum likelihood estimation

Let MLE be

\[
(\widehat\Theta,\widehat{\boldsymbol p}).
\]

paper written

\[
\left\|
\widehat{\boldsymbol\gamma}
-
T(Q,\widehat\Theta)
\widehat{\boldsymbol p}
\right\|_2
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Combined with the empirical marginal convergence, we get

\[
\left\|
T(Q,\Theta)\boldsymbol p
-
T(Q,\widehat\Theta)
\widehat{\boldsymbol p}
\right\|_2
\longrightarrow0
\quad\mathrm{a.s.}
\]

## identifiability closes the last step

If the population maps one-to-one, any parametric limit point that yields the same limit margin can only be
\((\Theta,\boldsymbol p)\). Therefore the main theorem supports

\[
(\widehat\Theta,\widehat{\boldsymbol p})
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}
(\Theta,\boldsymbol p).
\]

This logical chain is:

\[
\text{Empirical distribution convergence}
\;+\;
\text{MLE fits empirical distribution}
\;+\;
\text{The overall parameter is unique}
\Longrightarrow
\text{Parameters consistent}.
\]

## Asymptotic normality

The paper then briefly points out: Under standard canonical conditions, the asymptotic normality of MLE can be obtained by performing Taylor expansion of the log-likelihood at the true parameters, combined with the central limit theorem.

The original text does not single out formal theorems, information matrix formulas, or complete regularity conditions here. Therefore, the two layers of evidence should be separated when reading:

- strict identifiability has complete theorem and detailed proof;
- Consistency and asymptotic normality are explained as large-sample corollaries following the theorem.

## Boundaries and irregular points

Thesis hypothesis

\[
p_{\boldsymbol\alpha}>0
\quad\forall\boldsymbol\alpha.
\]

If the scale of some classes is zero, or the item parameters are at the equating/boundary position:

- Fisher information may be bizarre;
- The usual \(\sqrt N\) asymptotic normality may fail;
- The strict inequality relying on positive mass in the original theorem proof needs to be redone;
- The underlying structure itself may change.

## Limited sample meaning

Conformance description \(N\to\infty\). It does not give the following guarantees:

- How big is the bias under a certain \(N\);
- Whether rare attribute classes can be estimated stably;
- EM whether the global MLE is found;
- Is the standard error acceptable when approaching C2 failure?
- Whether the non-uniform item coverage generated by adaptive question selection satisfies the same convergence condition.

Actual analysis still requires checking multi-start stability, information matrix, class proportions, parameter boundaries and item coverage.
