## Two linear algebra tasks

### Task 1: Uniqueness within True Q

to start from

\[
T(Q)\boldsymbol p=\boldsymbol\mu
\]

The only recovery of \(\boldsymbol p\) requires full rank of \(T(Q)\).

Corresponding result:

- Proposition 6.1: \(T(Q)\) full column rank;
- Proposition 6.2: \(T_c(Q)\) full column rank;
- Proposition 6.6: Augment \(\widetilde T_{c,g}(Q)\) to full column rank.

### Task 2: Separability between different Qs

To restore Q, it is necessary to prove that the faulty candidate cannot replicate the true moments by adjusting the attribute distribution and item parameters.

Corresponding result:

- Proposition 6.3: The first \(k\) lines of the error candidate are still intact;
- Proposition 6.4: The first \(k\) lines of the error candidate are incomplete;
- Corollary 6.5: merge two cases;
- Proposition 6.6: Still separated after adding guess.

##Why column spaces are natural objects

After fixing candidate Q and \(\boldsymbol c\), allowing \(\boldsymbol p\) to vary in real number space, all linear combinations form

\[
\mathcal C(T_c(Q)).
\]

The set of moments generated by the legal probability vector is only a convex subset in the column space.

If a true moment does not belong even to the entire column space of error Q, then it certainly does not belong to the legal probabilistic convex set of error Q either. The authors demonstrate this stronger exclusion relationship.

## Proposition chain

### Proposition 6.1

Complete Q and saturated T allow extraction of block upper triangular square matrices, proving that \(T(Q)\) has full column rank.

### Proposition 6.2

\[
T_c(Q)=D_cT(Q).
\]

When each \(c_i\ne0\), \(D_c\) is full rank, full column rank is preserved.

### Propositions 6.3--6.4

For any \(Q'\not\sim Q\) and any candidate scaling parameter \(\boldsymbol c'\), prove

\[
T_c(Q)\boldsymbol p^*
\notin
\mathcal C(T_{c'}(Q')).
\]

The two propositions are split according to whether the candidate \(Q'_{1:k}\) is complete.

### Corollary 6.5

The two candidate cases are combined and \(c_i=1,g_i=0\) can be separated without noise.

### Lemma 6.7

If

\[
T_1\boldsymbol p\in\mathcal C(T_2),
\]

Then for D that is consistent in any dimension,

\[
DT_1\boldsymbol p\in\mathcal C(DT_2).
\]

The inverse proposition allows the author to prove the separation in the transformed simple space and then push it back to the original space.

### Proposition 6.6

Construct D that only depends on \(\boldsymbol g\):

\[
D\widetilde T_{c,g}(Q)
=
(0,T_{c-g}(Q)).
\]

The problem is reduced back to the guess-free situation, and Propositions 6.3--6.4 are then called.

## Prove the most valuable point of the strategy

The paper does not directly compare the complete likelihood of the two latent class models. It first projects the response distribution into a saturated joint positive response moment, and then studies it through the matrix column space:

\[
\text{Same structure}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{Moments represent the same ability to column permutation}.
\]

This set of "moment mapping-column space-consistency" structure later became an important language for the study of identityability of restricted latent class models.
