# Consistency comments, responses and method positioning

## Follow-up documents

Published in the same issue of *Psychometrika* in 2017:

1. Jingchen Liu, *On the Consistency of Q-Matrix Estimation: A Commentary*；
2. de la Torre & Chiu, *On the Consistency of Q-Matrix Estimation: A Rejoinder*。

This set of discussions breaks down the estimation problem implicit in the original paper into three parts.

## Question 1: Does the overall theorem depend on \(w\)?

Liu rewrites GDI using the full variance formula:

\[
\varsigma^2(\boldsymbol q)
=
\operatorname{Var}_w
\{E(Y\mid\boldsymbol\alpha_{\boldsymbol q})\}.
\]

monotonic relationship

\[
\varsigma^2(\boldsymbol q)
\le
\varsigma^2(\boldsymbol1)
\]

This holds true for any probability distribution \(w\). So the variance ordering of the theorem is quite robust to the concrete form of \(w\).

The strict inequality sign also requires that: the omitted true attribute cannot be completely predicted by the candidate retained attribute, and that it does change the success probability.

## Question 2: \(\widehat p\) in the sample depends on the initial Q

\[
\widehat p(\boldsymbol\alpha)
=
\frac{\sum_iw_i(\boldsymbol\alpha)Y_i}
{\sum_iw_i(\boldsymbol\alpha)}.
\]

Among them, \(w_i\) is generated by fitting provisional \(Q_0\). If the misconfiguration of \(Q_0\) seriously distorts the student posterior, \(\widehat p\) will have a non-negligible bias. The overall theorem cannot automatically eliminate this bias.

Liu therefore suggests:

- Use single-shot algorithms only when there is reason to believe that the initial measurements are accurate enough;
- Research iterative update \(Q_0\to Q_1\to Q_2\to\cdots\);
-Characterize the false equilibrium point and the region of attraction of the true Q.

## Author response: Practical robustness to a small number of misspecifications

de la Torre and Chiu believe that under small to moderate Q errors, the joint likelihood of the correct item can still restore the student posterior better. About 5% of the entries in the simulation are misspecified to support robustness under such limited conditions.

They also acknowledge that certain combinations of special misspecifications may cause greater posterior distortion. This is an empirical condition and does not form a general guarantee.

## Issue 3: Fixed consistency of \(\varepsilon<1\)

If the effect of a real attribute is weak, a missing hypothesis candidate may satisfy:

\[
\varsigma^2(\boldsymbol q)
>
\varepsilon\varsigma^2(\boldsymbol1)
\]

Even if the sample size approaches infinity, it will pass the fixed threshold. Liu proposed to let

\[
\varepsilon_n\to1,
\]

And give examples:

\[
\varepsilon_n
=
1-(\log n)^{-1}.
\]

Its approach to 1 is slower than the common \(n^{-1/2}\) estimation error, and can take into account both estimation noise and real small differences under certain conditions.

## Author's response to dynamic thresholds

When

\[
n=200\sim2000,
\]

This formula gives approximately

\[
\varepsilon_n=.81\sim.87,
\]

Significantly lower than the better performing .95 in the original simulation. Reaching .95 requires a sample size of approximately \(5\times10^8\).

The author points out:

- Asymptotic construction may not bring ideal limited sample performance to common sample sizes;
- Fixed .95 There is also no universal theory across situations;
- The threshold still needs to be studied under actual conditions.

## The iteration may also stop at the wrong equilibrium point

Even refitting with the proposed Q every round:

\[
Q^{(t+1)}=\mathcal A(Q^{(t)}),
\]

may still exist

\[
Q'\ne Q^*,
\qquad
\mathcal A(Q')=Q'.
\]

This explains why the current `GDINA` code detects loops and logs the non-convergence type. Iteration can reduce the initial Q dependence, but cannot establish consistency alone.

## Two types of goals

|target|focus|appropriate evidence|
| --- | --- | --- |
|Finite Sample Q Validation Tool|Keep correct lines and correct wrong lines under common \(N,J,K\)|Simulation, resampling, content review, external validity|
|Asymptotically consistent Q estimator| \(P(\widehat Q=Q^*)\to1\) |identifiability, threshold sequence, initial value condition, unified convergence proof|

The 2016 paper provides strong methods and experiments for the first type of goals; the second type of goals is proposed by comments and requires additional theory.

## Direct advice to users

1. Report the sources of initial Q and expected error proportions;
2. Check multiple groups of \(\varepsilon\) and mesa path;
3. Use bootstrap to observe the frequency of recommendations;
4. Compare single and iterative results;
5. Retain uncertainty for weak effect attributes;
6. Present the suggestion Q side by side with the content expert Q;
7. Use the refitted overall and question-by-question fits as supplementary evidence.
