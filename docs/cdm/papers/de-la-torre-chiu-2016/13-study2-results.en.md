# Study 2: Unconstrained G-DINA

## Design changes

Study 2 retains Study 1’s:

- \(N=2000,J=30,K=5\)；
- High-order attribute structure;
- Correct Q;
- 7/8 random entries are flipped;
- 100 replications；
- \(\varepsilon=.95\)。

The only core change is that the data are generated by unconstrained G-DINA.

## Success probability generation

The zero attribute group and the full control group for each question:

\[
p_0\sim\operatorname{Unif}(.1,.3),
\]

\[
p_1\sim\operatorname{Unif}(.7,.9).
\]

Their expectations are \(.2\) and \(.8\) respectively. The probability of success for the intermediate latent group goes from

\[
\operatorname{Unif}(p_0,p_1)
\]

is generated in and satisfies monotonicity:

\[
\boldsymbol\alpha\succeq\boldsymbol\alpha'
\Longrightarrow
P_j(\boldsymbol\alpha)
\ge
P_j(\boldsymbol\alpha').
\]

## Compare with Study 1

Under unconstrained G-DINA:

- The error vector correction rate dropped to 74.4%;
- The correct vector retention rate dropped to 90.3%;
- Correct entry retention rate remains at 98.0%.

Complex success probability profiles generate more close candidate GDIs, and entire row decisions are particularly sensitive.

## chance-adjusted improvement

Thesis report:

\[
42.8\%
\quad\text{at entry level},
\]

\[
39.4\%
\quad\text{at vector level}.
\]

Both were lower than the 72.1% and 71.1% of Study 1's worst conditions. The method still improves Q, but the magnitude of the improvement decreases with reaction structure complexity.

## Substantive explanation

If a property only has a small effect in a few interaction modes, the GDI penalty of missing it is likely to be small. Fixed \(.95\) threshold makes it easier to consider such candidates as good enough. Unconstrained G-DINA allows such local, non-uniform effects and is therefore more difficult to verify than DINA/DINO.

## Important gaps in design

The paper only changes the generation model and does not change the system at the same time:

- sample size;
-Number of attributes;
- number of items;
- Attribute correlation strength;
- Wrong way Q is concentrated;
- \(\varepsilon\)。

Therefore, Study 2 shows one aspect of the impact of complexity and cannot yet form a comprehensive boundary map.
