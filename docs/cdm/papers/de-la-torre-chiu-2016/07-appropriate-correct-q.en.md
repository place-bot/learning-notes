# appropriate and correct q-vector

## appropriate q-vector

Thesis definition: A candidate is appropriate if each latent group generated by a candidate q-vector has a homogeneous probability of success within it.

Let the candidate group be \(G_r\). Homogeneity requirements:

\[
p_j(\boldsymbol\alpha)
=
p_j(\boldsymbol\alpha')
\quad
\text{to all}
\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\alpha'\in G_r.
\]

## correct q-vector

Among all appropriate q-vectors, the one with the fewest attributes is defined as the correct q-vector.

This definition contains two goals:

1. Grouping is sufficient to explain the item success probability structure;
2. Delete attributes that do not contribute to the probability of success.

## Why are there multiple appropriate vectors?

If the correct vector is \(1110\), then:

- \(1110\) generates a homogeneous group;
- \(1111\) Further subdivide the homogeneous group, and the subgroups are still homogeneous.

So both are appropriate. correct defines the minimum number of attributes to break a tie.

## Why Weak Effects Cause Difficulties

The overall definition requires exact homogeneity. Only available in the sample

\[
\widehat p_j(\boldsymbol\alpha).
\]

As long as there is sampling noise, the subgroup estimates will usually not be exactly equal after adding irrelevant attributes; when a true attribute with a weak effect is missed, the GDI loss may be minimal. Practical algorithms therefore use approximation rules:

\[
\operatorname{PVAF}\ge\varepsilon.
\]

The lower the threshold, the simpler the model; the higher the threshold, the more attributes are retained.

## Statistical definition and content definition

Here correct is defined by conditional homogeneity and parsimony of success probability. It still needs to be contrasted with cognitive task analysis:

- A certain attribute may have a weak effect in the current sample;
- When attributes are highly correlated, one attribute may predict another;
- The defined attribute set may miss the real process;
- The same observational success rate structure may have different content interpretations.

Therefore, the paper calls the output suggested q-vector and requires domain experts to participate in the final judgment.
