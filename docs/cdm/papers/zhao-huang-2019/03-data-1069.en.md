# Raw data: 1,069 questions and nine attributes

## 1. Data sources and annotations

The paper uses 1,069 third-grade math problems. A panel of subject experts assigned one of the most salient cognitive attributes to each question, resulting in

\[
Q\in\{0,1\}^{1069\times9}.
\]

There is exactly one 1 in each row.

The paper does not provide:

- item source, year and region;
- Proportions of question types such as multiple-choice questions and fill-in-the-blank questions;
- Number of experts and professional background;
- Marking manual;
- Independent marking and discussion process;
- Expert consistency;
- Question stems and Q data files.

## 3. The distribution is highly unbalanced

The largest category `Operations of integers` has 666 questions, and the smallest category `Using calculators` has only 3 questions:

\[
\frac{666}{3}=222.
\]

If the nine-class model is directly trained, the smallest class has almost no independent samples that can be used for training, parameter adjustment, and testing.

## 4. Why does the paper cut data?

The author believes that low-frequency attributes can easily cause overfitting, so two categories are retained:

- `O`: Operations of integers, 666 questions;
- `M`: Mathematical thinking, 139 questions.

Total of two categories

\[
666+139=805.
\]

The remaining 264 questions were completely withdrawn from the experiment:

\[
1069-805=264.
\]

## 5. The meaning of data level

Pruning solves the problem that extremely small categories cannot be trained, and also changes the research tasks:

```text
Raw Data Targets: Nine Cognitive Attributes
                  │
             Delete seven categories of questions
                  ▼
Actual experiment goal: binary classification of O and M
```

Therefore, the experimental results can evaluate O/M discrimination, but cannot directly evaluate the recovery of the complete nine-column Q.

## 6. Representative unknown items of item bank

The language structure, course terminology, and question types of third-grade mathematics questions may have strong local characteristics. When there is no public data, the following questions cannot be verified:

- Whether the same question contains graphics;
- Whether graphical information is transcribed into text;
- Whether the attribute name appears directly in the question stem;
- Whether the same question template enters the training set and test set at the same time;
- Whether near-duplicate questions cause text leakage.

This information directly affects 85.2% of external validity.
