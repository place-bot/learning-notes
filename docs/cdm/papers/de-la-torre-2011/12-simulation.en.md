# Simulation experiment

## Research questions

The paper tests the question-by-question Wald test of A-CDM:

1. When the data is generated by A-CDM, whether the rejection rate is close to the nominal significance level;
2. When the data is generated by DINA or DINO, check whether A-CDM is found to be inappropriate.

Therefore, the simulated Type I error corresponds to the A-CDM true model; the power corresponds to the DINA/DINO true model.

## Fixed design

|Conditions|value|
| --- | ---: |
|Number of students \(I\)| 2,000 |
|Number of items \(J\)| 30 |
|Number of attributes \(K\)| 5 |
|Number of datasets for each generated model| 1,000 |
|Generate model| A-CDM、DINA、DINO |
|significance level| 0.01、0.05、0.10 |

The 30 questions are divided into:

- 10 single attribute questions;
- 10 dual attribute questions;
- 10 three-attribute questions.

The paper does not explicitly report the attribute profile generation distribution in the simulation paragraph. This item needs to be noted as an additional assumption when reproducing.

## Parameter settings

### A-CDM

The minimum and maximum success probabilities for all questions are 0.20 and 0.80.

|question type| \(\delta_0\) |main effect|
| --- | ---: | ---: |
|single attribute| 0.20 | \(\delta_1=0.60\) |
|Dual attributes| 0.20 |Each \(\delta_k=0.30\)|
|Three attributes| 0.20 |Each \(\delta_k=0.20\)|

### DINA and DINO

\[
g=s=0.20.
\]

So the low success group is 0.20 and the high success group is 0.80.

## Type I error result

When A-CDM is true:

|nominal level|Average rejection rate for dual attribute questions|Average rejection rate for three-attribute questions|
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.01 |
| 0.05 | 0.05 | 0.06 |
| 0.10 | 0.11 | 0.11 |

The rejection rate is generally close to the nominal level; under 0.05, the three attribute questions are slightly higher, and under 0.10, the two types of questions are both 0.11.

## power result

When DINA or DINO is true, at all three significance levels:

\[
\text{power}=1.0.
\]

According to this, the paper shows that under this set of simulation conditions with high signal and large samples, the Wald test can stably find the error reduction of A-CDM without significantly increasing the Type I error.

## What conditions cannot result be extrapolated to?

The experiment has not changed:

- sample size;
- Attribute distribution;
- Strength of effect;
- test length;
- A larger value of \(K_j^*\);
- Q matrix error;
- How sparse the pattern is.

Power equal to 1.0 reflects the joint condition of the current parameter separation and \(I=2000\), and cannot be interpreted as the Wald test always has full power under general conditions.
