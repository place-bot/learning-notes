# 7. Concept Supplement 13: IRTscale’s “Thermometer” Analogy

To better understand the characteristics of IRTscale, we can use a thermometer analogy.

## 7.1 Analogy of Temperature Measurement

Inspiration from the Thermometer Analogy

**Celsius vs Fahrenheit:**

- Same temperature, different value (0°C = 32°F)
- Conversion relationship: \(F = 1.8C + 32\)
- Important relationships remain the same (the boiling point of water is still 100 degrees above the freezing point)

**IRTscale analogy:**

- Same ability, different values (different anchors)
- Transformation relationship: linear transformation
- Important relationships remain unchanged (implications of ability differences)

## 7.2 Arbitrariness of zero point selection

Enlightenment of zero point setting

**Zero point of temperature:**

- Celsius: The freezing point of water is 0 degrees
- Fahrenheit: The freezing point of a certain salt water solution is 0 degrees
- Kelvin: Absolute zero is 0 degrees

**Zero point of IRT:**

- item anchoring: take the average difficulty of the item as 0
- Crowd anchoring: take the average ability of the crowd as 0
- Other anchors: any reference point of your choice

## 7.3 Maintenance of measurement quality

No matter what zero point and units are chosen, the essential relationship of measurement remains the same:

- Relative position remains unchanged
- The meaning of the difference remains unchanged
- The result of the comparison remains unchanged
